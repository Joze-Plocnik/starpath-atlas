    How to play & atlas sources

    Identify the constellation using only its stars. Use its full Latin name, such as Ursa Major; English translations and three-letter abbreviations are not accepted. A correct name earns one point and reveals the constellation's border.

    Then type a neighboring constellation's Latin name and select Place neighbor. Click or tap inside that constellation's region, outside the original one. Correct placement earns one point and reveals its border and name. Unknown neighbors stay hidden. Any point inside the named constellation counts; it need not be near the shared edge. Drag to pan, use +/− to zoom, or use arrow keys and Enter to aim on the map. Escape cancels a pending placement.

    Finding every neighbor advances to a new star-only round after a short pause. Scores add up across rounds; repeated answers earn no extra points. Reveal name and Skip remaining neighbors keep earned points, but add no points for skipped answers. New game resets the score. Changing the magnitude limit keeps your score; the bundled catalog supports limits up to 5.00.

    This is a north-up, east-left celestial atlas, independent of your location and time. Borders and neighbors use CDS VI/49 (Davenhall & Leggett; adjacency data by Bill Gray), defined in B1875 and precessed to J2000 for display. Serpens's two regions are treated as one constellation.
